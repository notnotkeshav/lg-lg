1. Introduction & How to Use This Manual

This manual documents the LG system — the platform that runs the complete air-conditioning sales, warranty, and Annual Maintenance Contract (AMC) business: from the first customer enquiry, through quotation approval, contract signing, invoicing and payment collection, to ongoing warranty/AMC service and contract renewal.

It is written for the people who use the system day to day — sales executives, branch/regional managers, service coordinators, call-centre agents, finance/billing staff, and dealers/technicians who interact with it indirectly through email links — rather than for developers. Wherever the underlying system does something automatically (an email, a status change, a calculation), this manual calls it out explicitly so you know what to expect and don't mistake automation for a bug.

How sections are organised The manual follows the natural order of the business process: Customer → Deal → Quotation → Contract → Invoice → Payment → Renewal, followed by the service/support side (Warranty, AMC, Tickets), then the management dashboard, bulk tools, and a troubleshooting/glossary reference at the end. If you only handle one part of the process (e.g. you are a service coordinator, not a salesperson), jump straight to Section 10.
Callout boxes used throughout Blue boxes are informational notes. Amber boxes (like this one) flag something you must do correctly or a step that is easy to get wrong. Red boxes flag a known system quirk or limitation — something the system does that looks like it should work differently, documented so you aren't caught off guard.

2. Platform Overview

2.1 What You'll See When You Log In

Everything in this manual lives inside one system, reached through your web browser. Day to day, you'll work in two main places:

  • The Desk — where all your data entry happens: Customers, Deals, Quotations, Contracts, Warranty, AMC, and Tickets are all screens here. Each one gives you a searchable list of records and a form to open, fill in, and save. This is where Sections 3–10 of this manual take place.
  • The Dashboard — a separate, read-only reporting screen at the /dashboard web address, for managers who want revenue/contract charts without opening individual records (Section 11).

You don't need to know how the system is built behind the scenes — this manual describes only what you see and do on screen.

2.2 The End-to-End Business Process

Almost everything in the system is a stage in one pipeline. Learn this flow first — every later section maps onto one box in this diagram:

CUSTOMER (CRM Organization) │ ▼ DEAL (CRM Deal) ─── Qualification → Spec-In → Proposal/Quotation → Negotiation │ → Won/Award → Ready to Close (or Lost / Lost by AM / Lost by RSM) ▼ QUOTATION (CRM Quotation) ── internal Zone approval matrix → customer approval → PO received │ ▼ CONTRACT (CRM Contract) ── signed agreement; generates the billing schedule │ ▼ INVOICE (via Uploader, type=Invoice) ── billing-schedule rows become real invoices │ ▼ PAYMENT (via Uploader, type=Payment) ── payments matched to invoices, contract marked Paid │ ▼ RENEWAL ("Renew" button → AMC Term → new Contract) once the old contract expires

Running alongside this sales pipeline is the service side: Warranty and AMC records, field Technicians and Dealers, and a call-centre Ticket Management screen that logs complaints, repairs, defects, escalations, and spare parts consumed — see Section 10.

2.4 How to Create Any Record on the Desk

Day-to-day data entry for everything in this manual — Customers, Deals, Quotations, Contracts, Warranty, AMC, Tickets, and all master data — happens through the same simple pattern on the Desk:

  1. Use the search bar at the top of the screen and type what you're looking for, e.g. "Deal", "Contract", "Warranty".
  2. Click + New from the list that appears, fill in the required fields (marked with a red asterisk), and click Save.
  3. Some records — Quotation, Contract, Warranty, Purchase Order — need one more step once you're happy with them: click Submit to lock the record in and trigger whatever happens next (emails, status changes, a billing schedule being generated, etc.), as described throughout this manual. Once submitted, a record generally can't be edited directly — you'd need to cancel and amend it instead.

Two other screens exist alongside this main workspace: the Analytics Dashboard at /dashboard (Section 11 — for viewing reports only, no data entry) and a technical interface used to connect a mobile app (Section 15, relevant only to administrators). A couple of older, less-used screens also exist at /frontend and /lgcrm — unless you've been specifically told to use one of these, the main Desk and the Dashboard are the two places this manual focuses on.

4. Customers (CRM Organization)

Every Deal, Quotation and Contract belongs to a Customer, represented by the CRM Organization record.

Key fields

FieldWhat it's for
Organization NameCustomer/company name.
Naming SeriesA human-readable customer code, built automatically from the first 3 letters of the name + city + creation date (e.g. abc-delhi-2026-07-08). You do not fill this in — it's generated on save.
Site Location City, Project, BranchSite and territory details. Project is free text here (not a link to the Project record).
Expected HP, Expected TonSizing estimates used during early qualification.
Warranty Expiry DateCustomer-level warranty tracking field (separate from the equipment-level dates on individual product lines).
Contracts InformationRead-only history table — automatically gets a new row every time a Contract against this customer is submitted (contract id, start date, expiry date, status). You never edit this by hand.

Step-by-step: creating a customer

  1. Search first. Because the system blocks you from saving a duplicate — the same organisation name + project + branch combination cannot exist twice — always search before creating a new record, even if the name looks slightly different from what you expect.
  2. Fill in Organization Name, city, project, and branch.
  3. Save. The customer code (Naming Series) is generated automatically.
  4. Add contacts, addresses, and any Customer Visit Form entries as the relationship develops.
Duplicate check If you get an error saving a new customer, it almost always means one already exists with the same name + project + branch. Search for it rather than trying a slightly different name to force it through — that just creates a second, harder-to-find record for the same customer.

Bulk customer import

Use the Import Excel action on the Organization list to bulk-load customers (and any attached Customer Visit Form rows) from a spreadsheet. The importer is flexible about column headers — it matches by label, a "scrubbed" version of the label, a known alias, or the literal field name — and it updates existing customers instead of duplicating them when a row's customer code (or name+branch) already matches a record. This means you can safely re-upload a corrected version of the same sheet.

The Project Module — Tracking a Customer's Site/Installation

A Project is a separate record from the Deal/Contract pipeline — it represents one specific customer installation or site (a customer can have more than one Project if they have multiple sites), and exists to give you a single, always-current answer to "is this site's cover still active?" without having to dig through that site's Deal history.

Key fields

FieldWhat it's for
Project IDThe Project's own record name. It must start with the letters "IL" — the system blocks saving and clears the field if you type anything else. New Projects default to "IL" for you to complete.
Customer Reference No., Customer NameLinks back to the CRM Organization; Customer Name is filled in automatically from it.
Vertical, Parent VerticalFilled in automatically from the customer's industry — useful for filtering Projects by business type.
StatusRead-only. One of IN Warranty / OUT Warranty / AMC Active / AMC Expired — you never set this by hand, it is written automatically (see below).
Expiry DateThe date this site's current cover (warranty or AMC) runs out — also kept in sync automatically.
Current OpportunityA hidden, read-only link to whichever Deal is currently considered this Project's "live" one.
Region, Branch, Branch Head NameThe servicing territory for this site.
Address / Shipping Address tabThe site's billing and shipping addresses.

How Status and Expiry Date stay in sync with Deals

You don't update a Project's Status or Expiry Date directly — they are pushed onto the Project automatically every time a Deal that's linked to it is saved: if the Deal has a Warranty Expiry Date, that date and the Deal's own Warranty/AMC Status are copied onto the Project; if it has an AMC Expiry Date instead, that's copied instead. A second, more careful check then re-confirms the update only if the Deal you just saved is genuinely the most recently created Deal for that Project.

Gotcha — this is also the cause of the Section 5 Project gotcha The first, simpler sync described above runs on every save of a linked Deal, with no check for whether it's the latest one — only the second check looks at recency. In practice this means editing an old, already-closed Deal for that site can still overwrite the Project's displayed Status/Expiry Date, exactly as flagged in Section 5. If a Project's status looks wrong, re-save the customer's actual current Deal for that site to put it right.

Turning an expired Project into a new Deal

Once a Project's Expiry Date has passed, a "Create AMC Opportunity" button appears on its form. Clicking it opens a new Deal, with the Deal Owner set to you, and the following pre-filled automatically from the Project:

  1. Customer and customer address are copied across.
  2. Branch and Region are copied from the customer record.
  3. The new Deal's type is set based on the Project's current Status: AMC Expired → "Lost AMC Conversion"; OUT Warranty → "Lost Warranty Conversion".

Review the new Deal, add/confirm its equipment (Product Details), and work it forward through the normal Deal → Quotation → Contract cycle (Section 5 onward) like any other opportunity.

Naming trap — this button does not create an "Opportunity" record Despite its label, "Create AMC Opportunity" creates a Deal, not a record in the separate Opportunity doctype described in Section 9.1. The two are unrelated features that happen to share similar wording — if you're looking for the customer's actual Deal from this button, check the Deals list, not Opportunities.

5. Deals & Opportunities (CRM Deal)

The Deal is where a salesperson tracks an opportunity from first contact through to a signed contract or a lost cause. This is the screen sales staff live in day to day.

Key fields

FieldWhat it's for
StatusQualification → Spec-In → Proposal/Quotation → Negotiation → Won/Award → Ready to Close, or Lost / Lost by AM / Lost by RSM.
Deal CategoryService (AMC/warranty-driven; the default) or Sales (new equipment). This choice affects which expiry date matters and which emails/renewal logic apply later — set it correctly at creation.
AMC Expiry Date / Warranty Expiry DateThe dates the whole AMC/warranty status calculation is built on.
Warranty / AMC StatusCalculated automatically — you cannot set this directly. If an AMC expiry date is present: shows "AMC Active" or "AMC Expired". Otherwise, if a warranty expiry date is present: shows "IN Warranty" or "OUT Warranty".
Product DetailsLine-by-line list of equipment on this deal (serial no, product, model, HP/tons, warranty/AMC type, dates, maintenance status).
Total HPCalculated automatically after every save by adding up the HP column across all Product Details rows.
From ContractSet automatically when this Deal originated from a contract renewal.
ProjectLink to the Project record grouping this customer's site history.

Step-by-step: working a deal

  1. Create the Deal from (or linked to) the Customer record.
  2. Choose Deal Category — Service or Sales — first; this is not easily changed later.
  3. Add every piece of equipment involved under Product Details, with serial number, model, and HP/tons filled in for each row.
  4. Move Status forward as the opportunity progresses: Qualification → Spec-In → Proposal/Quotation.
  5. At Proposal/Quotation stage, create a linked CRM Quotation (Section 6).
  6. Once a Contract is created and submitted from the quotation, the Deal is automatically updated with the final HP, AMC expiry date and annual revenue — you do not need to re-enter these.
Gotcha — HP must be a number Every row in Product Details must have a numeric value in the HP column. A blank or non-numeric HP on any row blocks the whole Deal from saving, because the system tries to sum the column into Total HP immediately after save. If a save fails with an HP-related error, check every product row, not just the one you just edited.
Gotcha — editing an old Deal can overwrite Project status Saving any Deal linked to a Project pushes that Deal's status and expiry date onto the Project record — even if it's an old, already-closed Deal and not the customer's current one. If a Project's displayed status looks wrong after someone edits an old Deal for housekeeping, the fix is to re-save the actual current/latest Deal for that project, not to hand-edit the Project.
Automatic "Lost" clean-up Every night, the system automatically marks a Deal as Lost if it has sat for more than 6 months past its warranty/AMC expiry date without reaching Proposal/Quotation or Won/Award. This is expected housekeeping, not a sign that someone closed your deal — check the activity log if you need to confirm when/why.

6. Quotations & the Approval Matrix

The CRM Quotation is a formal document — one you Submit once it's approved — with a built-in multi-tier internal approval process before it can be sent to the customer, followed by tracking of the customer's own acceptance and purchase order.

Key fields

FieldWhat it's for
ZoneGreen / Yellow / Orange / Red — a risk/discount tier. Auto-suggested from the deal's industry (via Vertical Master) and total HP against the HP & Zone Details table; you can override it if pricing genuinely differs.
Required ApprovalWhich approvers must sign off — AM only, AM+RSM, AM+RSM+Mr Alok, or AM+RSM+Mr Alok+MD — driven by the zone.
Start Date, End Date, Payment Term, Payment Frequency, Billing TermsContract period and billing cadence. Changing any of these automatically rebuilds the whole billing schedule (see below) — always set these before fine-tuning individual billing rows, not after.
Price Rate Per YearA comma-separated list of per-year rates for multi-year contracts with escalating pricing. Year 1 uses the main Amount field; year 2 onward pulls the next value from this list in order. If you don't supply enough values for every year, the remaining years simply reuse the base amount — so for an escalating contract, always supply one rate per year.
UploadedTick this once the customer's PO document is attached — the system will not let the quotation move on from "PO Pending" until this is checked.

The approval workflow

Open ──Submit for Approval──▶ Approval Pending ──Approve──▶ Approved Open ──Submit for Approval──▶ Yellow Zone Approval Pending ──Yellow Zone Approve──▶ Approved ──Yellow Zone Approve──▶ Orange Zone Approval Pending ──Orange Zone Approve──▶ Approved ──Orange Zone Approve──▶ Red Zone Approval Pending ──Red Zone Approve──▶ Approved ──Reject──▶ Rejected ──Reject──▶ Rejected Approved ──Send Quote to Customer──▶ Customer Approval Pending Customer Approval Pending ──Quote Accept──▶ PO Pending Customer Approval Pending ──Revise Quote──▶ Open PO Pending ──PO Receive──▶ Quote Won (quotation becomes locked/submitted)

Only users holding the matching Yellow / Orange / Red Zone Approver role can approve or reject a quotation sitting at that tier. A higher-risk zone routes through more approval stages before reaching "Approved".

Remarks are mandatory at rejection/approval points The system blocks you from moving a quotation out of certain states unless you have filled in the matching remarks field first (general Remarks, or the zone-specific Yellow Zone Approve Rejection Remarks / Orange ... / Red ... field). Always write your justification before clicking the workflow action button — the click itself will fail with an error until the remark is present.
Gotcha — re-saving a sent quote resends the customer email As soon as a quotation's status becomes "Customer Approval Pending", the system automatically emails the customer a PDF of the quote. This check runs on every save while the quote sits in that state — not just the moment it first gets there. If you need to make an unrelated internal edit to a quotation that's already been sent, be aware the customer will receive another copy of the quote email when you save.

Advance-payment reminders

If a quotation carries an Advance Amount and hasn't been submitted yet, the system checks every night for unpaid advance instalments and emails the quotation's owner with escalating reminders at 1, 20, 25 and 30 days overdue (government customers get an extra 60 days' grace). Ignoring these reminders long enough can flip the related contract's status to "Discontinue" automatically — see the caution under Section 15.

Turning a won quotation into a contract

Once a quotation is submitted (typically after "PO Receive" moves it to Quote Won), a "Create Sub Contract" button appears on the form. Clicking it generates a new CRM Contract pre-filled with the quotation's customer, dates, amount and billing schedule — you then review and submit the Contract (Section 7).

7. Contracts (AMC & Sales Agreements)

The CRM Contract is the signed, binding agreement. Submitting it is what actually starts the billing and service obligations — nothing before this point commits the company to anything.

Key fields

FieldWhat it's for
Contract StatusActive / Expired / Discontinue. Set automatically to Expired once Expiry Date passes — but the system never overrides a status you've set manually.
Contract TypeCalculated automatically on save: copied from the Deal's type for a brand-new customer relationship, or classified as "AMC Renewable" vs. "Lost AMC Conversion" by comparing today's date against the previous contract's expiry date plus 3 months.
Start Date, Expiry DateContract period — the system blocks saving if the start date is after the expiry date.
Dealer (table)Each dealer sharing commission on this contract, with their commission/contribution rate and their own accept/reject status.
Billing ScheduleThe full instalment plan carried over from the quotation (or regenerated the same way).

Customer PO IDUsed later to reconcile bulk invoice-Excel uploads against this contract.

What happens automatically when you submit a contract

  1. The Deal that produced this contract is updated with the final total HP, AMC expiry date (= this contract's expiry date), annual revenue (= this contract's amount), and a "duplicate trigger date" one month before expiry — used for renewal timing.
  2. Every dealer listed in the contract's Dealer table receives an email with a PDF of the contract (print format "AMC Offer") containing Accept and Reject links they can click without logging in.
  3. A new row is appended to the customer's Organization record (Contracts Information) recording this contract's id, dates and status.

If a dealer's Commission Rate is changed after the contract is already submitted, that specific dealer automatically receives a new Accept/Reject email about the updated commission.

Dealer accept/reject links

These links work without the dealer logging in. Clicking Accept notifies the dealer, the region's Region Head, and everyone holding the Finance role. Clicking Reject updates just that dealer's status on the contract. A contract that seems "stuck" is often just waiting on one dealer's response among several, not on the customer.

Gotcha — automatic renewal-deal creation does not run Older documentation and some code comments describe the system automatically creating a renewal Deal six months before a contract expires. This does not currently happen — do not rely on it. The working renewal path is the manual "Renew" button described in Section 9. Track upcoming expiries proactively (e.g. via the dashboard or a report) rather than waiting for an automatic prompt.

Nightly automated contract housekeeping

JobWhat it does
Expire contractsAny contract past its expiry date that isn't already Expired/Discontinue is automatically set to Expired.
Billing follow-upChases overdue, non-advance billing rows; sends an overdue-payment email at 30 days (60 for government customers) to the finance team, and automatically discontinues the contract past 60 days for either customer type.
Advance-payment remindersSame escalation pattern as the quotation version (Section 6), applied to the contract itself.

8. Billing, Invoicing & Payments

Invoices and payments are not created one at a time by editing a form — they are generated in batches using the Uploader tool, working directly off a Contract's billing schedule. Think of the Uploader as a "run an import" action rather than a permanent record you go back and edit.

Step-by-step: raising invoices

  1. Create a new Uploader record and set its type to Invoice.
  2. The system fetches the eligible billing-schedule rows and fills the Invoice Uploader table automatically.
  3. Review the rows — anything without a valid Contract ID, or that would duplicate an existing invoice, is skipped automatically.
  4. Save the Uploader. This is the moment the actual invoices are created — nothing is written to the system before you save.

For each row processed, the system creates a new Invoice record, splits it into instalment rows using the contract's Pay Term (each portion gets its percentage of the total and a due date offset from the billing date), and marks the originating billing-schedule row as "Invoice Raised", linked to the new invoice.

Step-by-step: recording payments

  1. Create a new Uploader record and set its type to Payment.
  2. The Payment Uploader table fills automatically from the source payment data.
  3. Save the Uploader. For each row, the system finds the matching invoice, applies the received amount to whichever instalment is due today-or-earlier and matches the portion amount, and recalculates the invoice's total paid/outstanding balance and status (Paid, or Partially Paid).
  4. The matching Contract Billing Schedule row is updated to reflect the same status, so the contract's payment history and the invoice always agree.
Gotcha — one bad row can affect the whole batch Both Uploader flows process every row in a single all-or-nothing operation. If one row in the middle of a batch fails (wrong contract id, mismatched amount, etc.), the whole save is aborted — but rows processed before the failure may already have been written. Always double-check your upload data before saving, and after any failed upload, verify what was actually created rather than assuming nothing happened.

Bulk reconciliation from Excel

On the Contract itself, an administrator can bulk-mark billing rows as paid by uploading an Excel sheet — the system matches each row's Customer PO ID against the contract to apply the update, rather than going through the Uploader tool.

9. Renewals

When a contract's status becomes Expired or Discontinue, a "Renew" button appears on the Contract form — but only if no renewal document already references this contract.

Step-by-step: renewing a contract

  1. Open the expired/discontinued Contract and click Renew.
  2. A new AMC Term document opens, pre-filled with the customer and a reference back to the old contract.
  3. Complete the AMC Term's billing details (payment term, frequency, billing terms, amount, start/end dates, billing schedule).
  4. From the AMC Term, generate the new CRM Contract. It is automatically pre-filled with the customer, industry, region, branch and company copied from the old contract, plus the payment term, dates, amount, frequency and billing terms entered on the AMC Term.
  5. Review and submit the new contract as normal (Section 7) — this starts a fresh billing/service cycle.
This is a manual process As noted in Section 7, there is no automatic renewal reminder built into the Contract itself. Use the dashboard or a saved report of upcoming expiry dates to identify contracts needing renewal, and action the Renew button proactively — don't wait for the system to prompt you.

Tracking renewal & upsell opportunities (Opportunity)

Separate from the Contract's own "Renew" button, Opportunity is a dedicated pre-sales record used by sales executives to actively work a pipeline of upcoming warranty-to-AMC conversions and AMC renewals, rather than reacting only once a contract has already expired.

FieldPurpose
Opportunity TypeWarranty or AMC — which kind of renewal/conversion this opportunity tracks.
StageQualified → Requirement Gathering → Proposal Creation → Proposal Sent → Negotiation/Review → Document Sign → Deal Won/Lost.
Opportunity StatusA finer-grained tag: Renewal, Warranty Conversion, Lost Conversion, Chiller, or AMC+TMS.

10. Service Operations — Warranty, AMC Records & Support

This section covers the post-sale service side of the business: handling warranty and AMC service requests, running the call centre, and tracking technicians, repairs, defects, escalations and spare parts.

10.1 Warranty

A Warranty record tracks a warranty claim/registration for a customer's equipment. It is a document you Submit once approved (naming pattern ID-###).

FieldPurpose

10.3 Ticket Management (Call Centre)

Ticket Mangement [sic — this is the actual name in the system] is the call-centre screen: one record captures an entire support interaction end-to-end, from the caller's identity through to parts consumed and the customer's satisfaction rating, rather than being split across several linked documents.

Equipment & warrantyModel number, date of purchase, warranty status and expiry.

Defect/repairDefect code and category/sub-category, repair end date/time.

Step-by-step: handling a call-centre ticket

  1. Agent takes the call and creates a Ticket Management record, capturing caller type and the type of call (Repair/Installation/AMC visit).
  2. Look up the customer, confirm equipment details (model, warranty status) — carried over from the customer's product history where available.
  3. Assign a dealer/technician zone; if the assignment needs to change later, the re-allocation fields track that history.
  4. Record the defect code/category once diagnosed, and any parts consumed during the repair.
  5. If the issue needs to go further (unresolved, disputed, or high-severity), set an escalation source/reason/type and route for the relevant approval (dealer, ASM, or technician sign-off).
  6. Capture the customer's satisfaction rating and any free-text feedback (VOC) once the ticket is resolved.
  7. Move Status to Closed once complete.

10.4 Technicians, Dealers & Field Visits

Technicians and Dealers are master records (Section 3) referenced throughout the service flow. Visit is a small summary record (first visit, most recent visit, number of chats/visits, average time spent, days visited) used to log a dealer's or technician's engagement history — it appears as the Dealer Visits table on the Dealer master. The Customer Visit Form is the customer-facing equivalent, recording a site visit's date, purpose (linked to the same Deal Type list used elsewhere), who accompanied it, and remarks — these rows are what the Organization bulk-import tool (Section 4) can also populate from a spreadsheet.

10.5 Repair, Defect, Escalation & Parts

These four areas exist as their own record types with dedicated fields — for example, Repair tracks a defect code, category/sub-category, symptom code, repair end date/time, and remarks; Defect tracks an item, a defect code (A/B/C), an attachment, and a Voice of Customer note; Escalation tracks source, reason, type, remarks, and separate ASM/dealer/technician approval checkboxes; Part tracks part code/name/category, consumed quantity, replacement type and warranty status of the part itself.

How these are used today In current day-to-day operation, the fields described above are most commonly filled in directly on the Ticket Management record (Section 10.3), which duplicates the same information in one flat screen for call-centre convenience. The standalone Repair/Defect/Escalation/Part records exist as independent masters/logs for anyone who needs to track this data outside a specific ticket (e.g. defect-code reference lists) — check with your service manager which one your team is expected to use for day-to-day logging.

10.6 Serial No — Per-Unit Warranty/AMC Tracking

Every physical installed unit can be tracked individually via a Serial No record: serial number, product/model, HP/tonnage, start date, expiry date, days left to expiry, and a Maintenance Status of Under Warranty / Out of Warranty / Under AMC / Out of AMC. A Type field (N/A / AMC / Warranty) indicates which coverage the record's dates represent. The near-identical Product Details table (Section 5) captures the same fields as line items directly inside a Deal or Contract — including a "dummy serial number" option for units not yet individually serialised.

Two ways to check the same thing Because Serial No and Product Details share the same fields, a unit's warranty/AMC status can be checked either from the master Serial No list, or by opening the specific Deal/Contract line where that unit was originally sold or contracted. Field/mobile-app users can also look this up per serial number via the Contract API (Section 15).

15. Mobile & External API (for Administrators)

This section is for whoever administers or troubleshoots the mobile app / external system integration, not for day-to-day CRM users.

The LG app exposes a generic, model-agnostic REST API (under /api/method/lg.api.master_api.*) that lets an external client (typically a mobile app) create and read records for any doctype by name, without a dedicated endpoint per doctype:

EndpointPurpose
create_recordCreate a new record of any model, given a JSON body with a Model name and a Data object of field values.
get_model_detailReturns the field metadata (labels, types, mandatory flags, options) for a given model — used by the mobile client to build its forms dynamically.
get_record_by_nameFetch one record by its ID.
get_all_recordsFetch all records of a model (name + display field).

CRM Deal creation is special-cased (an internal handle_crm_deal path) with its own stricter, field-by-field validation — mandatory fields, foreign-key/link resolution, and child-table handling are all checked explicitly against a field-definition list, rather than relying purely on Frappe's own doctype-level validation. If a mobile-originated Deal creation fails, check this validation path first rather than assuming it's a generic doctype error.

Keep the API contract in sync The authoritative request/response contract for this API lives in apps/lg/lg/api/api_document.md. Any change to these endpoints should be reflected there at the same time — the mobile app team relies on that document, not on reading the server code.

The dedicated Contract API

Separate from the generic model API above, Contracts have their own purpose-built API used by field/mobile apps: get_contract/get_contracts (filtered, paginated lookups), create_contract/update_contract/delete_contract, and — notably — submit_contract/cancel_contract, which let an external app drive a contract's submit/cancel workflow remotely rather than only through the desk UI. The same API also exposes get_customer, get_serial_numbers (searchable), and get_serial_details — so a field technician's app can look up a customer's equipment by serial number and see its warranty/AMC coverage on the spot during a site visit.

Two further admin-only tools worth knowing about: a bulk Deal export (System Manager role only) runs as a background job and lets an administrator download the entire Deals list as a file instead of paging through it on screen; and a small per-user list-column preference store remembers each user's chosen list-view columns between logins.

16. Known Quirks & Troubleshooting

These behaviours have been confirmed in the live system. None of them are things you did wrong — they are documented here so you recognise them and know the right response.

#What you'll seeWhat's actually happeningWhat to do
1 A Quotation already sent to the customer generates another copy of the quote email after an unrelated edit. The customer-notification email fires on every save while the quotation is in "Customer Approval Pending", not just the first time it enters that state. Avoid non-essential saves on a quotation once it's been sent; batch any small corrections together.
2 An expired contract never automatically produces a renewal Deal, despite this being described in older notes. The automatic renewal-on-submit logic is dead code and does not run. Use the manual "Renew" button (Section 9) and track expiries proactively via the dashboard.
3 A Quotation's own overdue-advance-payment breach appears to change the status of a same-named Contract instead of the Quotation. A known copy-paste issue in the underlying reminder logic. If you notice a Contract's status change without an obvious contract-side cause, check whether a same-named Quotation had an overdue advance payment. Report the mismatch to your administrator if it causes confusion; don't manually "fix" the Contract status without checking the source Quotation first.
4 A Project's displayed status seems to jump backwards after someone edits an old, unrelated Deal. Every Deal save pushes its own status/expiry onto its linked Project, regardless of whether it's the customer's current deal. Re-save the actual current/latest Deal for that project to correct the Project's display.
5 Saving a Deal fails with an error about HP. Every row in Product Details must have a numeric HP value — the system sums this column immediately after every save. Check every equipment row for a valid number in HP, not just the row you were editing.
6 A bulk Uploader (Invoice or Payment) fails partway through. All rows in one Uploader save are processed as a single operation; a bad row can abort the batch after some earlier rows already wrote data. Verify your source data before saving; after a failure, check what was actually created before re-running.
7 A Contract seems stuck in a pending state even though the customer has clearly agreed. Contract acceptance also requires each listed Dealer to click Accept on their own email — the contract can be waiting on a dealer, not the customer. Check the Dealer table's individual statuses before escalating a "stuck" contract.
8 A Contract Billing Schedule row behaves in a way that doesn't match what you can see/edit on the form. A handful of billing-schedule fields used by the reminder/billing automation (payment date, advance flag, discontinue flag, amount received, portion, outstanding amount, exchange rate, USD amount, "is free") are not exposed as editable fields on the visible form. Ask an administrator to check the record directly rather than assuming the visible fields are the complete picture.
9 Clicking "Create AMC Opportunity" on a Project opens a Deal, not something in the Opportunities list. The button's name is misleading — it maps the Project onto a new CRM Deal pre-filled with the customer, branch, region and the correct Lost AMC/Warranty Conversion type. It does not create a record in the separate Opportunity feature (Section 9.1). Look for the new record in the Deals list, not Opportunities.
